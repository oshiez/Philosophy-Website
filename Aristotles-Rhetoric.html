Aristotle's Three Pillars of Persuasion

Aristotle's three pillars of persuasion is about recognizing that to convey an idea to someone, it involves trust, reassurance, and proof. You can be perfectly logical, but if the audience or opponent views you as a liar, or a deceiver, that all goes out the window.

Ethos (Credibility, trust, and character)

Ethos is about proving you are competent, moral, and good willed. If somebody does not view you as someone reputable or good, your points will not convince anybody.

Credibility and experience (Phronesis)

Phronesis is about showing that you have expertise or experience in a certain topic. You should use Phronesis to show that you are credible and wise, and can accurately judge. If they think otherwise, they will see you as incompetent.

Character and morals (Arete)

Arete is about your morals and letting others know you have high standards, are honest, are responsible, and are fair. You should use Arete to show that your heart is in the right place, and you're a good person. If they think otherwise, they will think you are trying to deceive them.

Good will and kindness (Eunoia)

Eunoia is about showing the other person that you care for them and their wellbeing, and you're doing a thing out of the kindness for them. You should use Eunoia to show that you genuinely care for them, and that you're trying to benefit them. If they think otherwise, they will see you as selfish and wont support you.

Pathos (Emotion, Impact and Feelings)

Pathos is about giving the emotional aspect of your claim. You should use Pathos to make people feel a certain way about something and reinforce why they should care. If you do not provide good enough Pathos, people might be able to see and understand your claim, but they wont feel strongly about it.

Logos (Logic, facts and information)

Logos is about showing the informational side of things, like numbers and statistics. If you can not provide clear, concise, effective information, you will not convince anybody.
