Logical Fallacies

Logical fallacies are arguments that sound convincing, but are actually flawed. People may say logical fallacies by mistake or for manipulative purposes.

What are some common logical fallacies?

Ad Hominem

An Ad Hominem is an attack on the person instead of their argument. Instead of talking about a certain point their opponent made, they choose to focus on the opponent.

An example would be: "You cant talk about changing track and field. You have no legs!"

Straw Man Argument

A straw man argument is when someone makes someone elses position disproportionate. They stretch or change the original point so its easier to attack.

An example would be: "You dislike rollercoasters? So you're saying all rollercoaster rides should be demolished?"

False Dilemma

A false dilemma happens when a person provides only two scenarios without the chance for others.

An example would be: "You can cut this cake into 30 pieces, or you can cut it in half."

Appeal to Authority

An appeal to authority is made when somebody references an authoritative figure to back up their claim, whether it is right or wrong.

"An example would be: "My mama said football is the devil."

Appeal to Popularity

An appeal to popularity is made when instead of someone supporting their claims themselves, they base their claim off how a large group of people see it and accept that it must be objective.

An example would be: "Well, everyone in my neighborhood hates the taste of tomatoes, that's how I know they're not good for you."

Slippery Slope

A slippery slope is a fallacy where multiple claims are falsely supported by the one before it, turning into a snowball effect that doesn't reflect what would actually happen in real life.

An example would be: "If I don't pass this test today, I'll fail, then I'll get kicked out of school, then I'll grow up dumb, then I'll be homeless when I'm grown."
